*FDVU is the Norwegian term for facility management, operations, maintenance and development (forvaltning, drift, vedlikehold og utvikling). NS 3451 is the Norwegian standard that classifies building elements and technical systems.*

In [an earlier article](/articles/what-do-the-people-who-use-fdv-documentation-say/) I asked the people who use FDVU documentation every day how it works for them. The answer was clear: everyone had come across documentation that was outdated, wrong or impossible to find, and they spent on average more than an hour a week searching. In [another](/articles/why-digitalising-fdvu-pays-off/) I looked at what that costs. This article is about the third question: what do you do about it?

Since spring 2026 I have been working on getting the FDVU documentation for Boligbygg's properties into one shared structure. As of October, 406 buildings have been pulled out for structuring, and just over half are done. This is how we have gone about it, and what I would do differently.

The problem was rarely that documentation was missing altogether. The problem was that it was in *several* places, under *different* names, linked to the *wrong* system or the wrong building. Everyone who had added something had done it their own way, and every way made sense on its own. The sum was a structure nobody could explain.

## Why NS 3451

We needed a structure that wasn't *ours*. A home-made classification is always logical to the people who made it and baffling to the people who come after. NS 3451:2022, the Norwegian table of building elements and system codes, is the classification the industry already uses – in design, in cost estimates, in condition surveys under NS 3424 and in the FDVU systems themselves. When the documentation follows the same classification, a contractor can deliver straight into the structure, an operations technician knows where a ventilation manual belongs without asking, and a new colleague recognises it from their last job.

That is the point of a standard: it lets knowledge move with people instead of away from them.

**1. Structure and quality.** Building by building, we go through what is in the FDVU system, link documents to the right building element, correct names and remove duplicates. ## In short

- Use a structure the industry already knows. NS 3451 means the documentation can be read by everyone, not just by the people who made it.
- Define what "done" means before you start counting. Otherwise you are measuring a feeling.
- Clean-up isn't a project, it's a routine. The manual, contract requirements and training are what make the structure last.
