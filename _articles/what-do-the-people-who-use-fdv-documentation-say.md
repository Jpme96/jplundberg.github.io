*FDV(U) is the Norwegian term for facility management, operations, maintenance (and development) documentation for buildings.*

It's easy to talk about FDVU documentation in general terms. But how is it actually experienced by the people who use it every day? To find out, in October 2026 I ran a short survey among staff working in operations, maintenance, technical roles and management at a Norwegian public property manager.

**About the survey:** 8 people responded, and 5 of them have worked in the industry for more than 10 years. The sample is small, so the figures show tendencies, not statistical truths. But the answers point clearly in the same direction.

## Everyone has experienced the problem

Every respondent has experienced documentation that was outdated, wrong or hard to find. Asked how easy it is to find the right documentation when they need it, they gave an average of **4.6 out of 10**, and 7 out of 8 answered 5 or lower.

Several answers describe the same thing: documentation stored in many different places, under the wrong name or on the wrong asset, and drawings that aren't updated after alterations. When small jobs such as replacing a component are carried out, new documentation is often never uploaded.

## Time goes into searching

6 out of 8 spend at least half an hour a week looking for documentation, and 3 out of 8 spend more than an hour. Using the midpoint of each answer option, the average comes to around **1.3 hours per person per week**. Over a working year, that is about 60 hours per person spent just searching. It's a rough estimate, but it shows how quickly small losses in daily work add up.

## Versions and handover create uncertainty

7 out of 8 find that documentation exists in several versions or as duplicate files, without it being clear which one applies. Four of them experience this often.

The transition from construction to operations is another well-known problem. Everyone who had experience with it, 6 out of 8, has found that documentation from the construction phase doesn't match reality, for example as-built drawings. In addition, 3 out of 8 say that loss of expertise, for example when staff leave, has affected access to or understanding of the documentation.

Only 2 out of 8 think there are clear and well-known routines. The other 6 say the routines exist but aren't followed in practice. That may be the most important finding: the problem isn't that nobody has thought about it, but that the routines aren't sufficiently embedded.

The same theme recurs in the comments. Plenty of documentation is delivered, but it isn't enough to check that it has been delivered. You also have to check its quality, its structure and whether it actually describes what has been installed. Several respondents call for a shared understanding and a common structure based on Norwegian standards, so that everyone does things the same way.

## What is needed

Almost everyone thinks this matters. Asked how important it is to digitalise and structure FDVU documentation better, they gave an average of **8.6 out of 10**. 6 out of 8 answered 8 or higher, and half gave the top score.

When they ranked what matters most in a future solution, the answer was clear: **easy access** and **searchability** came first, ahead of security and backup. Mobile access and low cost came last. People first and foremost want to find what they need, quickly.

## My reflections

The survey confirms what I see in my own work. The challenge is rarely that the documentation doesn't exist. It's that it isn't structured, isn't up to date and can't be trusted. That's why I believe three things are essential:

- **A shared structure** that everyone follows, based on standards such as NS 3451 and NS 3456.
- **Quality control** of what is delivered, not just a check that something has been delivered.
- **Routines that are actually used**, with clear responsibilities and training.

The gains are time, fewer errors and better decisions. Read more about what digitalising FDVU can save in the article [Why digitalising FDVU pays off](/articles/why-digitalising-fdvu-pays-off/).
