*FDVU is the Norwegian term for facility management, operations, maintenance and development (forvaltning, drift, vedlikehold og utvikling). This article is based on Norwegian sources, all in Norwegian.*

When we talk about what a building costs, most people think of the construction project. But the bills don't stop when the keys are handed over. Throughout its life, the building has to be operated, maintained, repaired and developed. This is where FDVU documentation does its job, and where digitalisation can save the most.

## Operations are where the money goes

A guide from RIF (the Norwegian association of consulting engineers) and Norges Bygg- og Eiendomsforening shows that facility management, operations and maintenance make up **25–50 percent of the total costs** over a building's life, depending on the type of building. The share is highest for hospitals and lowest for homes. For office buildings, operations alone account for around two thirds of the FDV costs, with cleaning and energy as the largest items [1].

In other words: small improvements in how buildings are run have a big effect over time.

## What can be saved?

Menon Economics has estimated the potential gains from smart, digital solutions in Norwegian municipalities. Municipal property management had operating costs of **NOK 29.1 billion** in 2017, and Menon estimates that digitalisation could reduce these by **5–10 percent** by 2027 [2]. That is roughly **NOK 1.5–2.9 billion a year**, for the municipalities' own buildings alone (my own conversion of Menon's figures).

Looking at the whole building, construction and property industry, the Norwegian coordination council for digitalisation points to a savings potential of **around 20 percent, or just over NOK 100 billion a year** [3][4]. That figure covers the whole industry, not only operations, but it shows how much is at stake.

## The maintenance backlog makes it more urgent

According to Multiconsult's survey of municipal buildings, part of RIF's *Norges tilstand* (State of the Nation), **35 percent of Norway's municipal building stock** is rated as unsatisfactory, poor or very poor. The total upgrade need is estimated at **around NOK 168 billion** [5].

When money is tight, priorities matter more. You need to know what you have, what condition it is in and what is urgent. That is impossible without good, up-to-date documentation.

## Where do the gains come from?

The gains rarely come from one big change. They come from many small improvements in daily work:

- **Less searching.** When drawings, product data sheets and maintenance instructions are structured and searchable, time goes to the work instead of to finding the right document.
- **Planned instead of emergency maintenance.** With an overview of components, lifetimes and condition, maintenance can be planned before something breaks.
- **Better tenders and fewer surprises.** Diderik Haug at Statsbygg, the Norwegian government's building agency, put it like this: "Of course you get a lower price when you order, because you're not selling uncertainty" [6]. Contractors price in what they don't know.
- **Lower energy use.** Menon notes that operational optimisation with energy monitoring and building management systems in commercial buildings could alone save **1.1 TWh** [2].

## The gains don't come by themselves

It is important to be honest: digitalisation on its own saves nothing. In a report on realising the benefits of BIM, Standard Norge writes that the literature expects the biggest gains in FDV, but that today there is "very little evidence of this" [7]. Many buildings have digital systems, but the documentation in them is incomplete, outdated or organised in different ways.

That is why structure and quality matter at least as much as the tool:

- **A shared structure.** When documentation follows the same standard, for example the building element table in NS 3451, it can be compared, searched and used across buildings and systems.
- **Quality control.** Documentation that hasn't been checked can't be trusted. And documentation nobody trusts doesn't get used.
- **Clear ownership.** Someone has to be responsible for keeping the information up to date throughout the building's life, not just at handover.

## In short

Digital FDVU isn't about moving paper into a system. It's about making information about buildings so complete, correct and accessible that it actually gets used. Once that is in place, operations get cheaper, maintenance is better planned and decisions become safer.
