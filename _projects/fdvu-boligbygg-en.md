Good operations and maintenance depend on building documentation that is complete, correct and easy to find. At Boligbygg, I work to put this in place. So far, 406 buildings have been selected to bring their documentation into the correct structure, and we have reached 55% of the goal. Progress has risen steadily, from 9% in May to 55% in October 2026. The work has three focus areas.

## Why it matters

Poor documentation costs more than most people think. When information is spread across several places, formats and versions, work becomes slow and much of it is done twice. The wrong version of a drawing or instruction can lead to poorer operations and maintenance, and when drawings are missing, new ones have to be produced. Small losses in daily work add up to large amounts across a whole portfolio. Read more in my article on [why digitalising FDVU pays off](/articles/why-digitalising-fdvu-pays-off/).

## Structure and quality in the FDVU system

Documentation in the FDVU system is reviewed, cleaned up and quality-assured, so that the information about each building can be trusted.

## Shared practice

A revised FDVU manual and clear routines based on NS 3451:2022 make sure documentation is handled the same way across properties and projects.

## Skills

Training for the people who use the system every day, so that good structure is also used correctly.

How we have gone about it, and why structure is what keeps documentation alive, is the subject of my article [Structure is what keeps FDVU documentation alive](/articles/how-we-structure-fdvu-documentation-with-ns-3451/).
